# Stamp product spec

Locked from your choices in Stage 1. One number is a planning assumption and is marked as such: the manufactured cost that sits under the $5.00 shelf price.

## What a Stamp is

A Stamp is a 10 g disc of spread on a food-safe release film, nested in a printed card sheet. You peel one off and press it onto hot toast. The heat softens it into a layer. No knife and no open jar.

An unopened sheet lives in a pantry for 12 months. The home recipes below are kitchen prototypes, eaten from the fridge within 7 days. Only the industrial formulas are the products we sell, and only those claim the 12-month date.

## Who it is for

Someone who eats toast away from a full kitchen: a flat, an office, a hall, or a bag. They want a decided portion of flavour without carrying a jar or washing a knife.

## How to use it

1. Keep the sheet in a pantry.
2. Peel one stamp off the release film.
3. Press it onto hot toast.
4. Wait a few seconds. The residual heat lays the spread across the slice.

## Claims we will make

- Peel and press onto hot toast.
- One stamp is one portion, 10 g.
- Four flavours, sold as single-flavour sheets and a mixed sheet.
- No knife and no jar.
- Unopened sheet, 12 months in a pantry.

## Claims we will not make

- Health treatment, "better for you," low-sugar, organic, or gluten-free.
- That Salted Butter is a pat of fridge butter. The sold disc is a shelf-stable butter spread. In Australia and New Zealand the word butter on its own is a dairy-identity standard (milkfat, no added oil). The sheet shows the flavour name, then the word spread. The ingredient list names cultured butterfat.
- That Yeast extract is Marmite or Vegemite. Those names, jars, and trade dress do not appear on the pack or the site.

## Home prototypes

Each batch is eight stamps, 80 g. Shape into 10 g discs, press each onto a square of baking paper, and chill until they hold. Eat within 7 days. These batches test flavour and the peel-and-press gesture. They are not the shelf-stable product.

**Salted Butter.** 78 g unsalted butter, softened, beaten with 2 g fine salt.

**Raspberry.** 120 g raspberries, 30 g caster sugar, and 5 g lemon juice, cooked and stirred until 80 g of thick paste remains. Cool before portioning.

**Yeast extract.** 55 g yeast extract, 15 g tomato paste, 8 g black treacle, and 2 g salt, warmed and stirred into a paste that holds a disc. This prototypes the salty yeast flavour. The sold paste is the industrial formula below, not a scooped jar of an existing brand.

**Peanut.** 85 g roasted peanuts and 1.5 g fine salt, ground to a firm paste and portioned before the oil separates.

## Industrial formulas

Deposit 10 g ± 0.5 g onto release film, cut, nest eight discs in a card sheet, and flow-wrap. A failed weight check or a film that will not release is a rejected stamp, not a sheet that ships. Shelf life is 12 months, ambient, unopened.

**Salted Butter.** Cultured butterfat and salt at the same 2 g salt per 78 g fat as the home batch. Moisture is low enough that the sealed disc stays stable in a pantry and still softens on hot toast. No added oil, so the dairy description stays honest. On the sheet the flavour name is Salted Butter and the word under it is spread.

**Raspberry.** The home ratio (raspberry, sugar, lemon), cooked to the same thick paste and deposited at 10 g. Stability comes from the solids in the cook and the seal, not from a preservative claim. The label says it is made with 150 g of raspberries per 100 g, which is this cook: 120 g of fruit reduced to 80 g.

**Yeast extract.** Our yeast-extract paste, deposited at 10 g: yeast extract 80%, barley malt extract 15.5%, salt 2.5%, vegetable concentrate 2%. The salt share matches the home batch. Barley malt extract sits above salt, and vegetable concentrate sits below it, so the ingredient list stays in the order above. Contains gluten. The flavour name is Yeast extract. The pack does not say Marmite or Vegemite.

**Peanut.** Roasted peanuts 96%, vegetable fat 2.3% as the hard-fat stabiliser, and salt 1.7%, close to the 1.5 g salt in the home batch. The stabiliser keeps the disc from oiling off across 12 months. Contains peanuts.

The nutrition panels on the backs are calculated from these proportions and published composition figures. They are not a laboratory assay.

## Manufactured cost (assumption)

You chose the $5.00 shelf price. You did not choose a cost target. Until Stage 4 prices a real manufacturer, the spec uses this stack:

| | Per sheet | Per stamp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shelf price | $5.00 | $0.63 |
| Retailer keeps 35% | $1.75 | $0.22 |
| Stamp receives | $3.25 | $0.41 |
| Target manufactured cost | $1.50 | $0.19 |

$1.50 is 30% of the shelf price. The $1.75 left on Stamp's side of the wholesale price has to carry film, card, freight, and contribution. Change the 35% or the $1.50 and the recipes do not change; the Stage 4 manufacturer comparison does.
